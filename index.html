Jason DeWitt

I am a philosopher and senior lecturer in the Department of Philosophy at The Ohio State University. My research is in the metaphysics of science and pragmatism.

I am interested in how far a naturalistic metaphysics can be built from pragmatist starting points without giving up on realism, and in what becomes of deflationary and neopragmatist programs when they are asked to account for themselves.

Research

Research

My research develops a framework I call pragmatist realism. The driving question is how a naturalistic metaphysics of science ought to be built, and my answer joins pragmatist methodology to a qualified realism about scientific entities and relations.

This is meant as a third way between two familiar options. Traditional analytic metaphysics often carries excessive epistemological and metaphysical commitments. Neopragmatism, in deflating the representational pretensions of scientific discourse, can also deflate its objective aspirations. Pragmatist realism begins from the human perspective — from what our concepts do for creatures like us — without giving up on the world our sciences are about.

Publications

  • Pragmatist Realism about Causation Forthcoming
    Philosophy of Science

    Perspectivalists hold that what causes are is tied essentially to human agency. I argue that the pragmatist insight behind that view — that our concept of causation is bound up with our lives as deliberating, intervening agents — does not support the perspectivalist conclusion, and I develop an alternative on which causes are identified through human concerns while the causal relation itself stays objective.

  • Dogramaci’s Deflationism about Rationality 2021
    Synthese 199, 4437–4455

    Sinan Dogramaci derives a deflationism about rationality from epistemic communism, the view that epistemic terms function to coordinate a community around shared inferential rules. I argue that different sets of rules do not equally support that coordinative function, so deflationism need not be accepted even by the communist.

  • A Reflexivity Worry for Global Pragmatism

    Global pragmatism holds that any discourse can be given a functional-genealogical explanation of its social role. Using the neopragmatists’ own resources, I identify one discourse for which this cannot be done: the explanatory strategy itself, which cannot tell its story without employing the vocabulary it is trying to explain.

  • The Grounding Problem for Stance Voluntarism

    Stance voluntarism holds that realist and empiricist stances are alike rationally permissible, on the strength of a thin coherence norm. I ask what justifies that norm and from where, and argue that the most viable grounding commits the voluntarist to epistemic instrumentalism — a contested thesis assumed rather than defended.

In progress

  • The Zetetic Role of Mathematics in Science

    Mathematics contributes to inquiry prior to any explanatory payoff: it can constitute phenomena as objects of inquiry in the first place, individuating observations as instances of known structures and rescuing apparent noise as something worth investigating.

  • Local realisms and the historico-pragmatic method

    Examining episodes in which theoretical commitment or restraint demonstrably drove scientific progress, and arguing on practical grounds for local realisms and anti-realisms within particular domains.

Teaching

Teaching

I want students to leave my courses with a sense of philosophy’s importance to their lives. Insofar as philosophy is the critical evaluation of one’s fundamental concepts and values, and since those values inform everything one believes and does, philosophy is a way of becoming more fully oneself.

Two commitments shape how I run a classroom. The first is that philosophy is a conversation, which means small-group discussion, low-stakes entry points, and a room where a question is worth asking regardless of how far afield it seems. The second is that philosophy has been done well by people from every culture and demographic, and my courses are built to reflect that.
